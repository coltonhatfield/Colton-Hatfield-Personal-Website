How to read this assessment

PACER compares 16 curated plausible chains, not an exhaustive search or proof that an attacker can reach the target. “Feasible” means the chosen attack surface is in scope; account privileges, vulnerable software, asset placement, and actual response time still require validation. A purchased-access listing is not guaranteed to exist for this company or grant access to every asset shown in a path. ATT&CK mitigations connect to D3FEND techniques through MITRE's many-to-many bridge; a checked D3FEND box does not establish an independently measured probability of stopping an attack. Even a profile with every mapped defense selected can leave modeled paths profitable: controls reduce their estimated reach but are never treated as perfect blocks.

"Modeled path completion" is the product of assumed step-continuation chances. A step's percentage means the chance of continuing if the attacker reaches that step, after credited defenses. Neither number is a measured breach rate. Costs use Positive Technologies' 2026 listing medians and worked example where available. The $150 phishing panel is their one-month example; $250 per reached stage labor, 92% unopposed stage pass, defense effects, collection rates and fees are scenario assumptions. Listing prices can be reused across targets and are not a price for each ATT&CK technique. Code-repository theft uses only the entered intellectual-property value; its 20% realization is an assumption. The 88% breach-motive figure does not enter the calculation.

How PACER calculates profitability

Path equations

Path reach = product of modeled step-pass factors after credited defensesExpected payout = path reach × outcome-specific realizable valueExpected attacker profit = expected payout − upfront listings − reach-weighted effort − collection fee

Paths are ranked by expected attacker profit; the top five positive paths are shown. Each proposed control is evaluated alone against every candidate path, and ranked by how far it reduces the best remaining positive profit.

ATT&CK and D3FEND

There is no general one-to-one ATT&CK-technique ↔ D3FEND-technique mapping. ATT&CK techniques can have multiple mitigations; MITRE separately maps those mitigations to D3FEND techniques, often many-to-many and sometimes without a D3FEND match. The path-step links shown here are curated scenario alignments, not a complete ontology inference. Inspect MITRE's bridge ↗

Probability boundary

D3FEND identifies defensive behaviors, not measured efficacy. "Step continuation" means the assumed chance an attacker who reached a step gets past it after credited defenses. "Modeled path completion" multiplies those conditional chances across the path. PACER assumes a 92% unopposed continuation factor per step and applies capped, overlapping control credits scaled by the selected effectiveness setting. These are not observed detection rates or company-specific breach probabilities. The model does not separately measure alert quality, triage, or response latency.

Timing boundary

There are no reliable general averages for each ATT&CK technique or for time from initial access to attacker payout. PACER therefore does not invent per-step durations or sum them into a false full-path estimate. For ransomware paths only, it shows Mandiant's observed 2023 median of six days from initial access to ransomware deployment; this excludes negotiation and payment and is not a forecast for this company. Timing is not used in profit calculations. Mandiant timing analysis ↗

Economic boundary

Positive Technologies reports explicit underground listing prices for access, exploits, tooling, and infrastructure—not the total cost of executing an attack or a unique cost for each ATT&CK step. The model adds an assumed $250 effort cost when each stage is reached. Ransom, data, code-repository IP, cash diversion and double-extortion paths use separate payout formulas. Defender losses and annual attack frequency are out of scope.

Privacy and export

Inputs and uploaded profiles remain in browser memory. JSON and CSV exports are generated locally; they include scenario inputs and mapped paths, so handle downloaded files as sensitive.

OBSERVED

$7 customer-account resale value

An ARES 2023 peer-reviewed market study found a $7 median asking price for compromised online accounts; 91% were listed between $1 and $30. PACER retains $7 as a proxy ceiling and assumes 35% realization. These are not measured prices or sale rates for generic customer records; the sensitivity analysis tests $0.10–$7 proxy values.
